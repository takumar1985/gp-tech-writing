Why I Left the Big Banks. And What I See Now From the Outside.

By Timothy A., Consulting Technical Director at Greenwich Pacific Technology Partners

For most of my career I was inside. Big banks, big programmes, big politics. I ran infrastructure and technology programmes in institutions where a bad Tuesday could make the news.

I went independent because I kept seeing the same problem from the inside: institutions buy capacity when they need judgement. A programme gets in trouble, the response is fifty more contractors, and eighteen months later the trouble is bigger and more expensive.

From the outside, the view is clearer. Most of the value I've delivered as a consultant came in the first ninety days, and most of it was saying the quiet thing out loud. This architecture won't scale. That deadline isn't real. Your vendor is marking their own homework. Nobody inside could say it without career consequences. I could.

The other thing you see from outside: how much smaller institutions overpay for theatre. Big consultancies sell the logo, the methodology, the decks. What lands is a pyramid of juniors learning on your budget. The model works for them. It rarely works for the client.

I run a small consultancy on purpose. When you hire GP Tech you get the person who did the work, not the person who supervised the person who did the work. There's no pyramid to feed.

That's the pitch, honestly stated. Senior people cost more per day and less per outcome. Every client who's worked it out says the same thing afterwards: I wish we'd done this sooner.
