What 25 Years in Bank Technology Actually Taught Me

By Timothy A., Consulting Technical Director at Greenwich Pacific Technology Partners

A few things I believe after twenty-five years of this, stated plainly:

Most outages are self-inflicted. The vendor failure, the hardware failure, the freak event, they happen, but they're the minority. Most Sev 1s trace back to a change someone knew was risky, or a control everyone assumed someone else owned.

Documentation is a deliverable, not a courtesy. If the only copy of how it works is in someone's head, you don't have a system, you have a dependency on a person.

The second person is cheaper than the incident. Two people who understand the critical path cost less than one post-incident review.

Regulators are mostly reasonable. What they can't stand is being surprised. The firms that get in trouble aren't the ones with problems. They're the ones whose problems surface in the examiner's findings instead of their own reporting.

Cost cutting in a downturn is where the next incident gets funded. I've seen the cycle three times now. Budgets get squeezed, the boring resilience work gets deferred, eighteen months later there's a regulator letter. The maths never changes, only the names do.

And the biggest one: nobody cares about your technology. They care that it works. The best engineering I've ever been part of was invisible. Boring. Reliable. Undramatic. That's the job.
