The Questions Your Board Should Be Asking About Cyber (And Probably Isn't)

By Timothy A., Consulting Technical Director at Greenwich Pacific Technology Partners

I sit in a lot of technology-risk conversations. The board packs are polished. The questions aren't.

Most boards ask: are we secure? It's the wrong question. Nobody honest can answer yes, and nobody with a career answers no. What you get is comfortable ambiguity.

Here are the questions that actually cut through, the ones I've seen good non-execs use:

When did we last test recovery, end to end, and what broke? Not a tabletop. A real recovery, timed.

Which three vendors would hurt us most if they went down, and when did we last check their posture rather than their questionnaire?

Who owns the risk on this specific system, by name, and what authority do they actually have?

If we got hit tonight, who makes the decision to isolate, and have they ever rehearsed it?

What did the last penetration test find that we chose not to fix, and who signed that off?

That last one is my favourite. Every firm has unfixed findings. Mature firms have a list, an owner, and a date. Immature firms have a surprise waiting.

None of these require the board to understand the technology. They require the technology people to be honest with the board. Those are different problems, and the second one is where most firms are weak.
