Cloud Migration in a Bank Is Not an IT Project

By Timothy A., Consulting Technical Director at Greenwich Pacific Technology Partners

The fastest way to spot a cloud migration that will hurt: it's being run as an IT project.

I've watched this from inside and outside. The pattern repeats. Infrastructure gets a migration mandate and a date. Applications get lifted, shifted, and congratulated. Then month nine arrives, the bill is triple the estimate, the auditors are unhappy, and half the estate is back on the roadmap for replatforming.

Cloud in a regulated institution is an operating-model change wearing a costume. You're changing who can deploy what, who can see what data, how evidence gets produced for examiners, how incidents get contained. The technology is the easy part and it's the part everyone focuses on.

The questions that matter aren't technical. Can you show an examiner where the data lives and who can touch it, without a two-week archaeology project? Can you prove your controls survived the move, not just your workloads? When something breaks at 3am, does your runbook still make sense when half the estate is someone else's computer?

The programmes that worked treated migration as risk and evidence first, infrastructure second. Landing zones built with the controls baked in. Tagging and identity done before the first workload moved. Exit strategy written while everyone was still enthusiastic.

The ones that didn't are paying double: once for the migration, once for the remediation.
