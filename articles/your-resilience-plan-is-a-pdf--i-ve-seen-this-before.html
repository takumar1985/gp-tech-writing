Your Resilience Plan Is a PDF. I've Seen This Before.

By Timothy A., Consulting Technical Director at Greenwich Pacific Technology Partners

A bank asked me to review their operational resilience framework. It was ninety pages. Beautiful document. Colour-coded impact tolerances, a proper service map, board sign-off, the lot.

So I asked one question: when did you last prove it?

Silence. Then: well, we have the document.

The document said the payments service could tolerate four hours of disruption. Nobody had ever actually taken it down and watched the clock. The 'recovery procedure' for the critical dependency was a wiki page last edited by someone who'd left in 2021.

This isn't unusual. It's the norm. Regulators ask for resilience frameworks, so firms produce frameworks. Frameworks are easy to write and hard to argue with. Evidence is the opposite.

Here's the uncomfortable part. When an examiner or a client auditor digs in, they don't read the ninety pages. They ask for the last test, the results, and what changed afterwards. Three questions. Most firms can answer one and a half.

The fix isn't complicated, it's just work. Pick your most important service. Break it on purpose, in a controlled window. Time everything: detection, escalation, decision, recovery, client comms. Write down what went wrong. Fix the worst thing. Do it again in six months.

The banks that do this don't have better documents. They have better reflexes. Reflexes are what actually save you at 3am.
